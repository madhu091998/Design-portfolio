Simplifying contract management with AI

2025–2026 · Everstage · Product Design

I worked on the end-to-end design of Contracts at Everstage, alongside our product manager and engineers.

Contracts lets companies create and send personalized sales compensation agreements to their salespeople.

The project started as an effort to simplify a workflow that relied on DocuSign and a lot of manual data preparation. We eventually moved contract creation and signing into Everstage, with Eva, our AI assistant, helping populate contracts using data already available in the product.

The problem

Previously, Contracts relied on DocuSign, which was embedded inside Everstage.

Admins would upload a PDF, configure recipients, and use the DocuSign editor to place fields on the document.

But creating the template was only half the work.

Once the template was ready, admins still had to:

  1. Download a CSV containing the contract fields.
  2. Collect each salesperson's details from different sources and fill the CSV.
  3. Upload that data into a Datasheet within Everstage.
  4. Load the Datasheet into the contract and send the envelopes.

Most of this information, like quotas, commission details, and employee information, already existed in Everstage.

Admins also had to manually provide recipient emails and email content, and there was no way to preview each person's completed contract before sending it.

If something was wrong, they could end up voiding and resending the contracts.

Understanding the existing experience

I started by going through the entire workflow in the product, reviewing documentation and customer feedback, and studying how an existing customer configured their templates, recipients, and data.

We also worked with our customer success, implementation, and solution architecture teams to understand common customer setups.

A few things became clearer. Most contracts went to salespeople and their managers, recipient combinations varied by customer, and email content often followed common patterns.

These findings helped shape how we approached recipient selection, template reuse, and email customization.

Working through the design

I started exploring the high-level flow and reviewing different directions with the PM and engineers.

Why we kept templates and envelopes separate

One of our early ideas was to combine everything into a single contract creation flow.

But customers often needed to send the same contract to new salespeople joining midway through the year. With that approach, they'd have to clone the entire contract each time, even to send it to one person.

So we kept templates reusable, with the ability to create and track multiple envelopes from the same template.

Rethinking data mapping

Data mapping was one of the biggest unknowns when we started.

Initially, we explored letting admins select a data source and configure the fields and filters for every placeholder.

But some values weren't straightforward. For example, someone might need the ramp-adjusted quota for Q1 of 2026 from a particular quota category.

Supporting all these conditions manually would mean a lot of configuration.

Around the same time, our engineering team was developing the Contracts AI agent. Once it became reliable enough to generate queries from natural-language instructions, we started designing around that capability.

Instead of asking admins to configure every lookup, we could let them describe what they needed.

The challenge then became figuring out how admins would check whether AI had understood them correctly.

What I designed

The new experience has two main parts: creating a reusable contract template and sending envelopes using that template.

1. Creating a template

Admins can create a new template or clone an existing one, upload their document, and configure recipient roles, signing order, and reminders.

We also made recipient assignment easier. Instead of manually entering every recipient's email, admins can configure roles like reporting manager or a fixed compensation admin.

These recipients can then be populated automatically when sending contracts.

2. Populating fields with Eva

We used the native document editor developed by another internal team and extended the experience to support Contracts-specific data fields.

Admins can place placeholders on the document and describe what each one should contain.

For example, "Q2 quota from the New Business category."

Eva interprets these instructions, finds the relevant data sources, and generates a reusable query to fetch the values for each salesperson.

We also introduced pre-mapped fields as starting points, along with suggestions to help admins write better instructions.

3. Previewing and correcting AI results

One thing I spent time thinking through was how admins would know whether Eva had fetched the right values.

We introduced a preview step where admins can select sample salespeople and see the populated values in a table.

If something looks wrong, they can ask Eva to use a different source, add more context, or clarify what they need.

Eva can also ask follow-up questions when the instruction is unclear.

Admins can then preview the completed document to check both the values and the placement of placeholders before confirming the template.

The confirmed query is saved with the template and reused when sending contracts.

4. Creating and sending envelopes

Once the template is ready, admins can select the salespeople they want to send contracts to.

Other recipients, like their reporting managers, are automatically populated based on the rules configured earlier.

The saved query is then used to fetch placeholder values for everyone.

Admins can review the values in a table, make changes, and preview the documents before sending.

We also simplified email customization based on common customer use cases:

  • Same email for everyone.
  • Different email for each recipient role.
  • Unique email for each recipient.

Once sent, admins can track the contracts and see whose action is still pending.

Designing for when AI doesn't get it right

This was one of the more interesting parts of the project for me.

Eva can't always fetch the correct value, and there are different reasons why something might go wrong.

Sometimes the source has no matching data. Sometimes Eva needs more context. Sometimes the lookup itself fails. And sometimes the admin has intentionally chosen to enter the value manually.

We couldn't treat all these cases the same way.

Giving admins ways to correct AI

Admins can ask Eva to fetch values from a different source or modify how a value is calculated.

For example, they can ask Eva to divide the variable pay by 10 and regenerate the values.

But we didn't want admins to depend entirely on AI.

They can also edit values directly, copy and paste from spreadsheets, or look up values from an existing Datasheet.

For bulk updates, we let them match records using recipient email addresses and validate the values before applying them.

Handling missing and outdated values

Another case we had to consider was what happens when admins prepare contracts but send them a few days later.

The underlying compensation data might have changed in the meantime.

So we differentiated between missing and outdated values, with indicators, filters, and options to refresh the data.

We also allowed admins to proceed with missing values, but warned them that those placeholders might be blank in the final documents.

The idea was to make problems visible and give admins ways to resolve them without forcing them to start over.

Other things we worked through

There were quite a few iterations that didn't make it to the final experience.

One was how we configured roles and recipients. I explored asking for recipient roles later in the flow, once admins had selected the people receiving contracts.

But feedback from a solution architect and other designers showed that the approach introduced confusion and made some bulk-recipient scenarios harder.

We eventually kept role configuration at the template level and recipient selection at the envelope level.

Another change came midway through the project, when we moved from retaining DocuSign to using native e-signing. This meant revisiting parts of the experience and adapting the shared editor for Contracts.

A lot of the work involved balancing what would make the experience simpler with what was technically possible.

What changed

With the redesigned experience:

  • Contract creation and signing no longer depend on DocuSign.
  • Admins can reuse templates instead of recreating them for every batch.
  • Recipient relationships can be populated using existing Everstage data.
  • Eva can fetch contract values without admins manually preparing a CSV for every send.
  • Admins can review, correct, and preview values before sending.
  • Manual options remain available when AI can't provide the required information.
  • Contracts can be sent and tracked within Everstage.

The new experience was introduced through a phased rollout.

The recipient-side signing experience was developed by another team. Recipients can review, sign, and download their completed contracts through Everstage.

What I learned

This project changed how I think about designing experiences that involve AI.

It's one thing to design how someone asks AI to do something. It's another to think through what happens when AI misunderstands the request, returns the wrong information, or can't complete the task.

A lot of my time went into those cases: how people review the output, understand what went wrong, correct it, and continue without losing their work.

It also made me more conscious of where AI should help and where people still need control.

That's all for this one.
