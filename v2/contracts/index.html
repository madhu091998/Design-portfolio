Making contracts a little less complicated.

Redesigning how teams create and send hundreds of personalized sales contracts, with AI taking care of much of the repetitive work.

Role
Product designer
Timeline
2025 – 2026
Worked with
Product, engineering and the Eva (AI) team
I owned
The end-to-end flow, how admins work with AI, and the preview and review experience

01Context

One contract. Hundreds of different versions.

Every salesperson needs a contract outlining their compensation, targets, and other details. While the document itself might look similar, the numbers are different for each person.

For admins managing hundreds of salespeople, preparing and sending these contracts is a recurring task, especially when people join, change roles, or start a new plan year.

02Before

Digital contracts, manual work.

The existing experience was built around DocuSign. While signing happened digitally, getting contracts ready still involved quite a bit of manual work.

Admins had to create templates, download a spreadsheet of fields, manually collect and enter values for every salesperson, and upload the data again. And with no way to preview the completed contracts, mistakes could go unnoticed until they reached the recipients.

  1. Build the contract template in DocuSign
  2. Download the list of fields
  3. Collect and enter values in a spreadsheet
  4. Upload the values back into Everstage
  5. Send contracts without seeing the completed versions
  6. Discover mistakes, then void or resend documents

It also depended on DocuSign itself. Every admin had to connect their own account with the right permissions, field definitions lived only in DocuSign, and when something broke, support often couldn't see why.

03Reframing the problem

What if admins didn't have to collect all this information?

Most of the information admins were manually entering, like quotas, commissions, and compensation details, already existed within Everstage.

The problem wasn't that the information was missing. It was that people had to find it, organize it, and enter it all over again.

This opened up an opportunity to rethink the workflow, rather than simply recreating it without DocuSign.

How might we help admins prepare personalized contracts using the data they already have, without losing control over what gets sent?

Figuring out what AI should actually do.

AI could help find and fill values from data already available in Everstage. But contracts contain sensitive information, and a wrong number could mean sending someone the wrong compensation details.

So the challenge wasn't just getting AI to fill the values. It was designing an experience where admins could understand, review, and correct those values before sending anything out.

What AI could help with

  • Finding values from existing data
  • Translating descriptions into data lookups
  • Filling values for multiple people
  • Identifying values it couldn't retrieve

What admins should control

  • Deciding what each field should contain
  • Choosing when to use AI or enter values manually
  • Reviewing and correcting values
  • Confirming the contracts and sending them

04Design decisions

Let admins describe what they need

From configuring fields to describing them.

Previously, admins had to make sure their spreadsheet columns matched the fields in DocuSign.

With the new experience, they could instead describe what each placeholder needed in plain language, like “Q2 quota from the New Business category”. Eva would use that description and the available data in Everstage to find the appropriate value.

Typing / opens a data picker, so admins can point at the data they mean instead of relying entirely on an open-ended prompt. For values Everstage doesn't store directly, they can describe the calculation instead. And for information that couldn't be fetched this way, admins could still enter values manually, upload them, or set defaults.

Let admins check AI's work before committing

Making room to check before creating the template.

Just because AI finds a value doesn't mean the admin should have to trust it immediately.

We introduced a preview step where admins could select a few salespeople, see the values populated for each of them, and check how the final documents would look. If something was missing or incorrect, they could fix it before confirming the template.

Sample recipients matter because a description that works for one person can fail for another, for example someone who changed teams mid-quarter. Seeing the actual finished documents, not just a list of values, is what makes an admin confident the template is right. And fixes happen in place, so nobody has to start over.

Make reviewing hundreds of contracts manageable

Reviewing at scale, without losing the details.

Previewing a few contracts helps validate a template. But admins may eventually need to send contracts to hundreds of people.

So we also needed a way for them to review everyone's values together, spot missing information, and make corrections before sending.

The review table brought these values into one place, while still allowing admins to edit individual values, paste from spreadsheets, or look up information from datasheets. It can also be exported for review outside the product.

When AI isn't enough

Not everything needs to be handled by AI.

There would always be cases where information wasn't available, a value needed correcting, or an admin already knew exactly what to enter.

Rather than forcing everything through AI, we kept other ways to provide values and made missing information visible before sending.

The idea was to make AI helpful without making the workflow dependent on it.

Bringing it into Everstage

One less tool to depend on.

Alongside rethinking how values were populated, we also moved contract creation and signing into Everstage.

Admins no longer needed to connect individual DocuSign accounts or manage templates in an external editor. Templates, fields, envelopes, and signing could now be handled within the product, and a template can be cloned for the next plan year.

A few things I took away.

One thing this project made me think about was that designing an AI experience isn't just about what AI can do.

It's also about helping people understand what it's doing, knowing when to step in, and giving them control when something doesn't look right.

Sometimes, the most important part of designing with AI is figuring out what should still be left to people.
