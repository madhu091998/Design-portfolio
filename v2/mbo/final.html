New module, built from scratch

Sales teams don't only reward revenue. Many also pay for goals like getting certified or running great demos, called MBOs. Everstage customers managed these in spreadsheets and email.

I designed the MBO module from scratch, so companies can set these goals, have managers score them, and pay on them, all inside Everstage.

What's an MBO?

MBOs (Management by Objectives) are goals that aren't a sales number. Managers score them each period, and the score decides part of someone's pay, usually 10 to 35%.

For example:

Jordan scores Alex 85 on a quarterly demo scorecard. The plan says 80 or more pays a $500 bonus, so Alex earns $500.

The problem

Customers already ran MBOs, but outside Everstage. Every period:

  1. 1
    Managers scored their teams in Excel or email.
  2. 2
    Admins chased each manager and merged everything into a CSV.
  3. 3
    The CSV was uploaded into Everstage to calculate pay.

Admins became middlemen, repeating this every period, with no audit trail and no visibility for managers or payees.

Who it's for

Four people are involved, and each gets one home in the product.

The core challenge

These scores turn into real money. And a manager scoring their own team may be tempted to score high.

So the question behind every screen was:

How do we make sure only fair, reviewed values ever reach someone's pay?

The answer shaped the whole module: drafts stay private, a second person approves (a maker-checker process), and only approved values flow into pay.

How a score becomes pay

Programs and commission plans stay separate. Publishing a program creates a datasheet, and the commission plan reads from it like any other data.

Designing the experience

Four flows, one for each person.

01Admins set up a program

A guided, step-by-step setup: the period, who's in it and who scores them, the goal form, and optional stages (Goals, then Scores) with approvals and lock dates. Problems like a missing manager show up inline, before publishing.

02Managers score their team

Managers only see the people they score. They can save drafts anytime, which nobody else sees, then submit when they're sure. Bulk edit and paste from a spreadsheet help with big teams.

03Approvers review

Approvers see a snapshot of exactly what was submitted. They approve, or reject with a comment and it goes back to the manager. Approval is per person, so one rejection never holds up the team.

04Payees and admins see the results

  • My MBO

    Payees see their goals and official scores, never a draft. Before, they only saw a final number on their statement.

  • Org-wide MBO

    One screen for admins to answer "are we ready to pay?", with gaps, fixes and a full history of who changed what.

Key decisions

  • Programs, not plan add-ons

    MBO started as a form attached to a commission plan. It became its own program, with its own period, people and approvals.

  • Draft, then submit

    Managers can save freely without affecting pay. Payees only ever see official values.

  • Approval per person

    One rejection never blocks the rest of the team.

  • The last approved value stays

    A resubmission never wipes out someone's pay mid-review.

  • Managers can change mid-year

    Admins set an effective date, without touching the reporting hierarchy.

  • Warn, don't block

    Locking a payout with unfinished MBOs shows a warning and a link to fix them.

What changed

One place to set, score, approve and pay on MBOs, instead of spreadsheets and email.

What I took away

Building from scratch meant there was no screen to improve, only a messy real-world process to understand. Because every value ends up in someone's pay, most of my time went into what's a draft, what's final, and who can change what.
